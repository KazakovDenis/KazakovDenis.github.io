# Content Workflow

## Purpose

This document defines the content lifecycle for Denis Kazakov's personal blog, from capturing raw ideas to publishing and distributing finished articles.

The goal is to build a sustainable writing practice with minimal overhead, preserve potentially valuable ideas, and develop the strongest ones into full-length articles.

Writing style and editing requirements are defined separately in `.agents/rules/article-editing.md`. Follow those rules whenever drafting or editing content.

## Content formats

Use three levels of content maturity.

### 1. Raw ideas

Raw ideas are unpolished observations, hypotheses, questions, opinions, or lessons learned from engineering management, software development, leadership, and professional life.

Examples:
- An observation about a recurring team problem.
- A question about the future of engineering management.
- An unexpected consequence of an organizational decision.
- A disagreement with a commonly accepted engineering practice.
- An experience that might lead to a broader conclusion.

Raw ideas do not need an introduction, a conclusion, or a publication-ready structure. Preserve the original meaning and context without prematurely turning every idea into an article.

### 2. Short posts

Short posts communicate one clear thought, observation, or argument. They are intended to be published independently and may later become the starting point for a full-length article.

A short post should:
- Focus on one central idea.
- Be understandable without additional context.
- Prefer a concrete observation over generic advice.
- Be concise and require little preparation.
- Leave room for discussion when appropriate.

A short post is not necessarily a teaser for an article. It is a valid standalone format.

As a starting point, aim for 3–10 sentences. Do not enforce a strict length if the idea requires more or less space.

### 3. Full-length articles

Articles explore a subject in greater depth. They may develop an existing short post, combine several related ideas, analyze a real experience, or introduce an entirely new argument.

An article should provide substantially more value than simply expanding a short post with filler. Depending on the topic, it may include:
- Context and the problem being addressed.
- A detailed argument and supporting examples.
- Personal experience or lessons learned.
- Counterarguments, limitations, or alternative perspectives.
- Practical conclusions or implications.

Do not turn every raw idea or short post into an article. Develop an idea when it has enough substance to justify deeper treatment.

## Idea lifecycle

Use the following lifecycle as a flexible default:

1. Capture an idea with minimal friction.
2. Clarify its central point without overdeveloping it.
3. Decide whether it is worth publishing as a short post.
4. Publish the post when appropriate.
5. Revisit the idea if it generates meaningful discussion, reveals a deeper question, or remains interesting to the author.
6. Develop selected ideas into full-length articles.
7. Publish and distribute the finished article.
8. Link related content where doing so helps readers discover the broader discussion.

Not every idea must pass through every stage. An idea can remain a private note, be published as a short post, or be developed directly into an article.

Do not treat engagement metrics as the sole criterion for deciding which ideas deserve further development. The author's interest, professional relevance, originality, and potential for deeper analysis also matter.

### Short posts

LinkedIn is the default channel for publishing short professional posts.

Substack Notes may be used as an additional channel for short observations when it makes sense to build an ongoing presence on Substack.

Do not automatically publish every short post to every channel. Avoid unnecessary cross-posting and formatting work.

The personal website does not need to host every short post. Publish short-form content there only when it serves a clear archival, navigational, or editorial purpose.

Habr is primarily intended for complete, substantial Russian-language articles rather than miscellaneous short observations.

### Full-length articles

For a full-length article, use this default publication sequence:

1. Prepare the Russian original.
2. Prepare an English translation and adaptation.
3. Publish both language versions on the personal website. The website versions are canonical.
4. Publish the English version on Substack.
5. Publish the Russian version on Habr.
6. Announce the article on LinkedIn, adapting the announcement to the platform rather than merely copying the article introduction.
7. Consider Telegram distribution if the channel exists and the article is relevant to its audience.

The author may change the sequence or omit a channel when there is a good reason.

## Language and adaptation

Russian is the default language for drafting new full-length articles.

The English version should preserve the original argument, intent, and personal voice while reading naturally to an English-speaking audience. Translate and adapt rather than mechanically translating sentence by sentence.

Short posts may be drafted directly in either language, depending on the intended audience and publishing channel.

Do not create two language versions of every short post by default. Translate or adapt a short post when there is a clear distribution benefit.

## Editorial principles

- Prefer a consistent, sustainable publishing practice over an ambitious schedule that creates unnecessary pressure.
- Optimize for the value and originality of ideas, not the volume of published content.
- Preserve potentially valuable observations before deciding whether they deserve publication.
- Treat short posts as complete content in their own right, not merely promotional material.
- Use short posts to explore ideas that may later justify deeper analysis.
- Do not manufacture controversy or optimize opinions solely for engagement.
- Do not expand a short post into an article without adding meaningful depth.
- Avoid duplicating content across channels when a link or adapted announcement would work better.
- Keep the author's real experiences, opinions, and reasoning at the center of the content.
- Do not invent personal experiences, results, metrics, quotations, or claims to make a piece more convincing.

## Working with the AI agent

When helping with content:

1. Identify the current stage: raw idea, short post, or full-length article.
2. Preserve the author's original intent and distinguish personal experience from hypotheses or general claims.
3. Recommend the smallest useful next step rather than automatically proposing a full article.
4. If an idea is promising but underdeveloped, help clarify the thesis or identify the questions that need answering.
5. If a short post already communicates a complete thought, do not expand it unnecessarily.
6. When an article is justified, help identify the central argument, relevant examples, and missing evidence before drafting.
7. Follow `.agents/rules/article-editing.md` for writing and editing.
8. When relevant, identify related existing ideas or articles to avoid duplication and uncover opportunities to connect content.
9. Do not publish, cross-post, or modify canonical content without explicit authorization from the author.

When uncertain about the intended format, prefer preserving the idea and asking one concise clarifying question over making irreversible editorial decisions.

# Article editing rules

These rules apply when drafting, reviewing, shortening, or formatting posts in `content/posts/`.
They capture Denis's editorial preferences; the specific request in the current task still controls
the scope of an edit.

## Keep the author's voice

- Preserve the author's point of view, opinions, experiences, and level of certainty. Keep first-
  person details and real examples when they explain why the author believes something.
- Prefer plain, direct language and concrete observations over polished slogans or abstract
  summaries. Do not make every paragraph sound equally tidy or turn a personal essay into corporate
  guidance.
- Avoid stock AI rhythms: neat antitheses (`X, not Y`), paired clauses built to sound quotable,
  repeated restatements of the thesis, predictable three-part lists, and generic transition or
  conclusion sentences. In particular, do not add lines like “The formatting looks finished; the
  thinking may not.”
- Keep natural variation in sentence length and phrasing. Do not add deliberate errors or awkwardness
  to make prose appear human.

## Make English sound idiomatic

- Check collocations and ordinary usage, not just grammar. Rewrite wording that a fluent English
  speaker would find unusual, vague, or hard to follow.
- Preserve the article's spelling variety consistently. Denis's current English copy uses British
  forms such as `analyse`, `summarise`, and `organise`.
- Keep conversational contractions where they fit the author's voice. Avoid making every sentence
  formal or impersonal.
- Do not silently rewrite a direct quote as if it were the speaker's exact wording. If a quoted
  phrase sounds unnatural and its exactness is unclear, ask or flag a proposed edit.

## Edit for focus, not for minimum length

- Remove duplicated explanations and throat-clearing, but keep the example, counterargument, or
  qualification that gives a point substance.
- When asked to shorten, preserve the main argument and the author's distinctive examples. Do not
  compress each section into a generic takeaway or force an arbitrary word count. The AI etiquette
  draft was shortened from roughly 1,600 to roughly 1,100 words by removing repetition while
  keeping its five principles and workplace anecdotes; use that as precedent, not a fixed target.
- Make each section add something new. Watch for the introduction, numbered principles, and
  conclusion explaining the same idea in turn.
- Keep headings distinct. Avoid repeating the same keyword or question in multiple headings when a
  more specific heading can show how the later section advances the argument.

## Preserve intentional references and details

- Do not remove or normalize company, product, or platform references unless asked. If wording seems
  odd, first check whether it refers to a real interface, signature, or in-joke. For example,
  “Sent using @Claude” is a deliberate reference to the signature Slack adds to messages sent
  through Claude; its effect on how readers approach a message is part of the article's point.
- Do not invent personal anecdotes, research, statistics, quotes, or claims. Preserve the author's
  meaning and attribution; flag factual uncertainty instead of silently changing it.
- Keep AI-use disclosures accurate and specific. When Denis asks to disclose AI editing, say that he
  wrote the first draft and used AI to revise or shorten it; do not imply that AI wrote the article.

## Structure and formatting

- A short `TL;DR` may appear near the top when requested. Keep it to the article's actual main points,
  with one concise item per principle; do not copy the full section headings as a second table of
  contents.
- Use Markdown headings for the article's argument. For a post with five principles, number the five
  principle headings consistently and make sure the summary uses the same principles in the same
  order.
- Use blockquotes for a genuinely central quotation. Do not add decorative formatting to ordinary
  prose just to make the page look busier.
- Keep front matter, cover image fields, and other publishing metadata intact unless the request
  includes them.
